AYZA POS

A point-of-sale system for Windows that keeps selling when the internet does not. Built for small retail in South Africa and Ethiopia, where a till that needs a connection is a till that stops.

How it is built

Offline first

Every sale is one atomic SQLite transaction: the sale, its lines, the payment split, the stock deduction and the stock movement all commit together or not at all. There is no network call anywhere on the path between scanning an item and the drawer opening.

The database lives in the application support folder, not beside the executable, so a desktop shortcut, a reinstall or an update never separates a shop from its sales.

Money

All amounts are handled as integer cents. Floating point never touches a total, a split or a change calculation.

Card is capped at total − cash. Cash may overpay and produce change; card may not. The cap is enforced in the UI and again before the sale is written, because the first is a courtesy and the second is the rule.

Box pricing

One product sells as a single piece or as a full box, each with its own price, out of one shared stock count. The two live as separate cart lines over the same product, and stock is checked across both before either can grow.

At checkout every line is converted back to pieces, with the box saving carried as a discount. Stock, reports and refunds stay on a single unit no matter how it was sold.

Printing

Receipts and 50 × 32 mm barcode labels go to Xprinter hardware through raw Win32 printing — dart:ffi into winspool.drv — so ESC/POS and TSPL reach the printer as bytes instead of as a rendered page.

Printing is deliberately off the critical path. A receipt that fails goes into a queue that survives a restart, and the next customer is served immediately. A printer problem must never hold up a till.

Licensing

Lifetime licences are signed offline with Ed25519 and bound to the machine's Windows GUID. The private key never leaves an encrypted vault on the issuing computer; the app ships only public keys and verifies without any server.

Keys are additive: a new signing key is added to the app's trusted set rather than replacing the old one, so rotating a key can never lock out a shop that is already running.

Who did what

Cashiers, supervisors and the owner see different screens. Refunds, price changes, stock reductions and user management are each gated on a permission, and every sale, removed line and cleared cart is recorded against the signed-in user rather than a generic admin account.
